What the things can become. Plan only what these very things can really be made into with hand tools and an ordinary kitchen; a project that needs anything ruled out below is a different project.
- Sawn timber (boards, battens, planks, plywood, offcuts): cut to length, square across or at an angle (a mitre where two parts meet at a corner, a bevelled end); shaped with a saw — a taper, notches, rounded corners, or an outline cut with a jigsaw or coping saw (a curved bracket, an arched top, a scalloped edge); drilled, screwed, nailed, glued, clamped, sanded. Parts may stand at an angle: a leaning side, an A-frame, splayed legs. Not sawn along its length (ripped), planed thinner, carved, bent or turned.
- Logs, branches and firewood: round or split, uneven, bark on. Cut square across into shorter lengths or rounds (a round's cut faces are flat; nothing else on a log is); stood on end, laid, stacked; drilled and joined with long timber screws or dowels. Not sawn along the length into boards, planed flat, hollowed or turned: a seat, a table top or a shelf from logs is a round cut across a thick log, or several logs side by side, never a "board" cut from one.
- Food: washed, peeled, cut, mixed, cooked, served, in real amounts.
- Yarn: crocheted or knitted with a hook or needles that suit its weight, in a real pattern: rows, rounds, granny squares, hexagons, flowers with petals, amigurumi, tubes — and pieces made of several motifs joined (a granny-square bag, a hexagon cushion).
- Strands (rope, cord, string, twine, wire, ribbon, cable, pipe cleaners, straws): cut to length, bent at an angle or into a hook, coiled flat into a spiral (a mat, a coaster, a basket's base), closed into a ring (a bracelet, a hoop, a ring toss ring), tied with an overhand (stopper) knot, twisted from a doubled strand into a cord, threaded with beads, glued or stitched turn to turn. Not braided, wrapped round another object, or tied in any knot but the overhand: a project needing those is a different project.
- Toy bricks: only the bricks in the photo, each once, joined on their studs.
- Flat sheet stock: cut into two-dimensional parts with explicit sizes, outlines and positions within the source; every physical copy is separate. Joined along declared matching paths, folded where supported, or changed into a shaped assembly through a bounded demonstration with persistent before/after states. A finished object may combine many parts and intermediate assemblies; do not restrict suggestions to a single piece or a short sequence. A sheet's width and height matter: do not account for it as a length of timber, and do not apply saw kerf to scissors.
- Everyday things (books, binders, cups, cables, paper, cloth, clips): placed, stacked, propped, filled, folded, bent, rolled or tied, as the thing allows.

What the film can show, and so all a plan may ask for: putting things in place (on, in, under, between, against, across several supports, leaning, tilted, splayed); cutting a part across its length into lengths, and shaping a wooden part (any outline, a taper, notches, rounded corners, angled or mitred ends), or food into cubes, slices, bits, wedges or strips; pouring, sprinkling, stirring, heating; a tool at work (saw, drill, screwdriver, hammer, sander, brush, glue, clamp, pencil, square, level, tape measure, knife, whisk, spoon, ladle, scissors, needle); stitches of crochet or knitting; a change of shape by hand (bending, folding, rolling, coiling, knotting); any other hand operation drawn as a short demonstration. Sawing along a length, planing, carving, hollowing, turning, welding and painting a picture cannot be shown.

Sizes people expect. Use them; when the things are too small for one, make a smaller thing that is still right (a plant stand or a low side table, not a 20 cm "chair").
- Seats: a chair or stool seat 43–48 cm high and at least 28 × 28 cm; a bench 43–48 cm high, 25–35 cm deep; a footstool or step 20–35 cm high; a boot bench 40–48 cm high.
- Tables: dining or desk 72–76 cm high; side table 50–65 cm; coffee table 38–48 cm.
- Shelves and storage: shelves 15–30 cm deep; 25–35 cm between shelves for books; an 18 mm board spans at most 80 cm without sagging; a wall shelf hangs on two brackets or fixings at least.
- Planters: soil at least 15 cm deep; holes in the base.
- Standing up: a free-standing thing's base at least a third as wide as it is tall; legs at its corners or ends, set in no more than a fifth of its length; three legs for an uneven floor.
- Fixings: a screw is the fixed part's thickness plus at least half (better two thirds) of the part it goes into, and shorter than the two together (a 2 cm board into a 4.5 cm leg: 5 cm screws; a 12 cm log into a log: 20 cm timber screws); drill pilot holes in hardwood and logs; at least two fixings a joint so it cannot turn.
- Food, a person: 75–100 g dry pasta or rice, 120–150 g meat or fish, 150–250 g vegetables; a can is about 400 g, an onion 150 g, a garlic clove 5 g.
- Yarn: DK takes a 3.5–4.5 mm hook, worsted 5–5.5 mm, bulky 6–9 mm; a flat circle in single crochet starts with 6 and adds 6 a round.
- Toy bricks: 0.8 cm a stud, a brick 0.96 cm high, a plate 0.32 cm.
