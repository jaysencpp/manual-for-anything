Many crafts have their own diagram language, and their makers follow it more exactly than words: a crochet or knitting symbol chart (each stitch its standard symbol, worked round by round or row by row), origami's fold diagrams (valley and mountain lines, the standard arrows), a knot's over-and-under drawing, a sewing pattern piece (grainline, notches, seam allowance), a cutting diagram (every part laid out on its board or sheet, the offcut hatched), a wiring diagram, a planting plan, a quilt block's layout. A recipe's amounts, times and heat are in its captions; a panel would repeat them, so most recipes have none. If no step gains from one, answer {"panels": null}.

What a panel must be:
- Exact, from one source of truth. What to draw comes first from the operation facts (each step's quantities, sites and joints, given at the end), then from the script's objects and parts; the captions only explain them, and where a caption seems to disagree, draw the facts. Every count, size and order matches (a round of 12 stitches shows 12 symbols; a fold shown is the fold the step makes). Never draw what the script does not say. Use repeat elements referring to those operation IDs; the engine derives repeated marks and quantity labels from their sites/counts.
- In the craft's own symbols, one for each distinct action, placed where it really goes: a crochet chart's increase is its own symbol (two stitches fanning out of one), each stitch sits over the one it is worked into and the rounds touch; an origami valley fold is dashed and a mountain fold dash-dotted, each with its arrow; a cutting diagram's parts are to scale on their board.
- Cumulative, where the craft's diagram is: what earlier steps made stays, drawn in the ink at a lighter weight or alpha; what this step adds is drawn in the ink at full strength, its newest marks picked out in the accent, and the engine reveals its repeated marks as that step progresses, so the panel grows as the animation does. Nothing else in it moves: no drift, pulse or loop; at a given p it is the same picture.
- Readable at a glance: one clear diagram, centred, filling the area; a key of at most three symbols where the diagram uses symbols ("× sc", "○ ch"), text at least 18 px. No title inside: the engine writes each step's label above the area.
- Plain: no jokes, no decoration, no characters.
