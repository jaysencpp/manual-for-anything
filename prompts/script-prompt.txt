Name places on the sheet as it lies at that moment, seen from above ("top" is its far edge): a corner "top-left"; a side's middle or a point "top"; the "centre"; an edge "left edge", a slanted one "top-left edge"; the "centre line" (far edge to near edge through the middle) or "horizontal centre line"; or a crease an earlier fold named. "move": what moves (a corner, a point or an edge); "onto": where it lands (a point, an edge or a line); "through": the point the fold passes through when a corner goes onto a line (else it folds through where that line meets the edge nearest the corner); or no "onto" and "cm": an edge folded in by that much. "crease": "valley" (the default: the flap comes up and over) or "mountain" (it folds behind); "unfold": true folds it and opens it again, leaving the crease; "layers": "top" folds only the uppermost layer (one wing of a plane), else every layer; "name": what later folds call this crease; "move":"over" turns the sheet over, left to right; "move":"rotate" with "deg" (90 a quarter turn clockwise) turns it flat on the table. Two corners or two wings are two actions, one each, in the order they are done. A dart's nose: {"v":"fold","k":"paper","move":"top-left","onto":"centre crease"}, then the same with "top-right"; a sheet folded in half lengthways and opened again: {"move":"left edge","onto":"right edge","unfold":true,"name":"centre crease"}. "label" only a size or count shown at the fold ("2 cm"). A fold at an angle: "move" the edge that turns, "through" the point it turns about, "deg" the angle between that edge and the fold line, no "onto", and "label" the angle ("30°") (the edge lands at twice that: a straight edge folded in thirds about its middle is 60° one side, then 60° the other). Cutting through the folded layers: {"v":"cut","k":"paper","with":"scissors","shape":[[u,v],…]}, one shape an action, 3 to 24 points as fractions of the folded sheet's box as seen from above (u from its left, v from its far edge; a little past 0 or 1 cuts a notch out of an edge) — every layer under it is cut. "move":"open" opens the sheet out flat again, undoing every fold since it was flat, and shows what the cuts make. A six-pointed snowflake from a square: top-right onto bottom-left; top-left onto bottom-right; "top-right edge" through "top" at 30°; "top-left edge" through "top" at 30°; then cuts across the wedge's wide end and notches along its sides; then open. Origami, paper planes, snowflakes, cut-out chains, cards, napkins and envelopes are made of folds, in the plan view.
- "strand": k, a strand — wire, rope, string, twine, ribbon, cable, a pipe cleaner, a straw: something long and thin that bends — is bent, coiled, closed into a ring, knotted, twisted or threaded with beads, one operation an action, "op" naming it. Give it "part":{"family":"rod","size":[length, thickness, thickness]} (length in cm, the thickness as it is: a wire 0.2, a rope 1, a ribbon its width). The film works out every operation exactly from the strand's length and thickness, so what the caption says is what is drawn: never draw a bent strand, never give it states with appearances. It starts straight, seen from above, held still at the end the operation holds; the place along it is "at": "middle", "start", "end", "6 cm from the end", "a third", "40%" or a number of cm from the start. The operations:
  "bend": the part beyond "at" turns by "deg" (positive to the right walking from the start, negative to the left, or "side":"left"|"right"; at most 180 — a hairpin, a strand folded in half, is 180 at "middle"), as a curve of "radius" cm (default tight); "hold":"start"|"end" says which end stays (the shorter side moves by default). A bend that would make it cross itself is refused. Wire hooks, loops, zigzags, stems, hangers and clasps are bends: "6 cm from the end" 90, then "6 cm from the start" −90.
  "coil": the last "cm" of it (all of it if none) wound flat into a spiral, "radius" the hole in the middle, "side" left or right; "n" the turns if you say them — the film works them out from the length and thickness and refuses a count the length does not make (100 cm of 1 cm rope is about 6 turns). Cables, rope, hoses, ribbon and flat spirals.
  "ring": the ends brought together into a ring, the last "cm" (default a tenth of it, at most 3) lying over the first: a bracelet, a hoop, a wreath base, a hair tie, a ribbon loop. Its circumference is its length less the overlap.
  "knot": "name" a knot tied at "at": "overhand" (a simple knot, a stopper) or "figure-eight" (a bulkier stopper that jams less and unties easily), each taking about 15 to 16 strand-thicknesses of it and tied as near an end as it fits (a stopper knot "at":"end"). A step that only ties a knot is filmed close on the knot, so give a knot its own step and say in its caption how the end goes (over, under, back through). Tie it on the straight strand, before coiling or bending it: a knot made where the strand is curved lies across the turns and is refused. No other knot can be drawn: a plan needing one (a bowline, a square knot, a hitch, a whipping) says what it does instead, in a step with "reshape" or in words.
  "thread": "n" beads of diameter "cm" (bigger than the strand), "gap" cm between them, "color" ("blue, white" repeats the pattern), centred on "at" (default the middle); they ride on the strand through every later operation, so a necklace is a thread then a ring.
  "twist": a strand folded in half (bend 180 at "middle", first) twisted by "n" turns into a cord (it takes up some length, which the film works out and the cord's length gives). A cord is then a strand of its own: bend it, ring it, knot it.
  "straighten": all of it flat again.
Never "demonstrate" a strand: a hand operation with no operation above (pressing a coil flat, pulling a knot tight) is a "use" with no tool, or left out. Glue or stitching on a strand itself (a coil's turns held together) is a "use" with no joint. "label" only a size or count the caption gives ("3 cm", "9 beads"); the angle, turns, lengths and counts are shown without it. Jewellery, ties, hangers, cables, cords, wrapped or hung things and any long thing that bends are made of strands, in the plan view; a strand cut to length first ("cut" into pieces with "cm") gives strands of that length.
- "demonstrate": any other hand operation that is not a change of shape: "intent" the actual operation, "focus":{"k":"piece","bounds":[0,0,1,1],"clearance":0.1}, "demo":{"actors":[declared keys],"draw":"(ctx,p,env,scene) => { ... }"}, plus requires/effects. It replaces those actors' drawing for 3.2 s; p runs 0–1. scene.point(key,[localX,localY]) gives an actor's working point on stage, accessible as point.x/point.y or point[0]/point[1] (local units are cut-out pixels × 4); scene.point01(key,[u,v]) gives the same from fractions of the actor's box (u from its left, v from its top, 0 to 1): use it for any position given as a fraction or measured across the thing. A tool that does the work is an actor too: put its key in actors and move it with scene.draw. scene.draw(key) keeps the whole actor at its current pose. With explicit x AND y, scene.draw places its local origin [0,0] there unless anchor is supplied (anchor:"center" is supported); with partial overrides it retains the current anchor. Only canvas operation marks are clipped to the focus; whole actors stay visible beneath them. Locate stitches with scene.point(key,[localX,localY]) INSIDE the focus, never at [0,0] unless the operation is actually at that corner; env gives font/mono, dimensions and palette. Plain bounded canvas drawing only: each actor drawn at most once a frame, no filters, gradients, globals, randomness, async or unbounded loops. Show the actual transfer, tool or stitch; if no faithful demonstration is possible, choose another idea.
Any act can carry "n" (how many times: four legs, eight screws) and "label" (the quantity it is done with, shown at the work: "8 mm", "4 × 40 mm", "20 min", "100 g", "15 cm"). Every number with a unit in a caption is in an act's label, cm or n. Something is placed before it is cut, and a `to` is placed before anything is poured, sprinkled, stirred or heated in it. Tools are placed like objects (a pot "place" with rel "floor").

How the film checks it (operation contract version 2):
- Every action has a unique "id" and an "intent" (normally its verb; "use" takes fasten, drill, glue, sand, clamp, mark, apply or fit). Each step lists in "claims" the ids its caption speaks of, and promises nothing those actions do not show.
- Counted work gives explicit "sites":[{"x":0.1,"lat":-0.3},…] on the named face (x 0–1 along it, lat -0.5–0.5 across it); the sites are the count, and "n", if given, equals their number per object. Every step with an action that puts in fasteners ("part") has "caption":{"template":"Drive {count} screws through the top","actions":[that step's fastening action ids, and only those]}: the film counts the sites and writes the number.
- Every attachment has a joint in the root "joints": {"id":"joint-a","a":"part-worked","b":"receiving-part","method":"fasten|glue|clamp|fit","face":"upper","otherFace":"upper","sites":[…],"depthCm":5}. For fasten, face is a's outward entry face (the tool goes in through a into b) and otherFace is b's face touching a; for glue, clamp and fit they are the mating faces and face each other. The use that makes it gives "joint":"joint-a", k:a, to:b and the same intent. A fastener's tools entry gives its real length in "cm", and depthCm equals it: longer than a's thickness, no longer than a's and b's together (the sizes above). Screws are driven with a screwdriver or drill, nails and dowels with a hammer, bolts with a wrench. Glue goes on before the parts close. Plan cuts and positions so these faces really meet: the film reports a joint that misses rather than move parts to hide it. Bricks join on their studs: no joints for them.
- States: an object, tool, made piece or cut part can declare "state":"packed","states":[{"id":"packed","label":"Wrapped"},{"id":"unwrapped","label":"Unwrapped","appearance":{"width":100,"height":40,"shapes":[{"d":"M0 0 H100 V40 H0 Z","fill":"#f2dca0","stroke":"#1d2b4f"}]}}] (bounded static SVG paths, at the object's size). Actions give "requires":[{"k":"food","state":"packed"}] and "effects":[{"k":"food","state":"unwrapped"}]; every effect has its prior state, and the new look stays. Root "goals" name final states the steps reach. Record real preparations only (a wrapped block is unwrapped before it is cubed).
- "focus":{"k","bounds":[left,upper,width,height],"clearance":0.1} in the part's normalized coordinates frames the contact area and tool for a close-up; joint work focuses on its sites by itself.

Before answering, check, and fix what fails: the design's size meets the expected sizes for what it is; every part in the design is cut or used whole by the steps under the same key and length, and each object's parts fit in it; every operation is one these materials allow and the film can show; each fastener is longer than the part it passes through and shorter than both parts; every structural part is fixed and the thing stands; every number in a caption is in the actions; the finished thing really does what the purpose says.

Every fastening action MUST name all three references: joint (the connection), with (the driving tool), and part (the declared screw/nail/dowel/bolt). For example {v:"use",intent:"fasten",id:"drive-front",k:"front",to:"left",joint:"front-left",with:"screwdriver",part:"screws"}. A screw listed in tools is not automatically selected by an action. Use the joint's sites for fastening positions, never path or otherPath: those describe flat-sheet stitching/tool paths. The fastener's cm must equal joint.depthCm. Provide the counted caption for its sites.

For a simple open box or planter, declare assembly:{kind:"open-box",id:"box",base:"base-key",front:"front-key",back:"back-key",left:"left-key",right:"right-key",fastener:"screws",driver:"screwdriver"}. These are five distinct actual parts created by explicit cuts or used whole. Include explicit place actions in base, front, back, left, right order. The assembly compiler sets their final placement relations: base flat on the floor, sides on the base's front/back edges, ends between the sides. End length equals base width minus both side thicknesses. The geometry checker still rejects impossible dimensions and joints. Declare the named fastener and driving tool. All cuts and fastening operations must be shown explicitly.

Prefer named cuts for rigid stock: cuts:[{id:"cut-a",stock:"board-a",with:"saw",parts:["front","back","left","right"]}]. Lengths and source keys come from design.parts, one physical copy per key. Do not duplicate lengths or offcuts in this declaration. Show the operation in a step as {id:"saw-a",v:"cut",intent:"cut",k:"board-a",cut:"cut-a"}. We compile its with, into, labels and offcut using 0.3 cm saw kerfs. Place the source stock first; every named cut must be shown exactly once. Each stock object can be allocated to only one named cut. A declaration without an execution step creates nothing. Do not repeat inline into on a named cut; these fields are derived. Raw cut.into remains available for other cutting operations.

Every physical response explicitly gives cuts and assembly. Use cuts:[] if no named cuts are used. Use assembly:null for a different construction or manually specified joints. When actions reference named cuts, cuts must contain every referenced declaration. When using generated box joint names, assembly must contain the complete box id, role keys, fastener and driver. Do not omit assembly and leave joints:[] while referencing generated joints: there is then no declared geometry to compile.

A place action gives rel or to. Without rel, an explicit to key means on that support, and to:null means floor. Compiled assembly members take their final relation and support from assembly, so their placement actions may omit repeated rel when they give to. Every referenced support must still be placed first.
